I'm a Principal Applied Scientist on the Responsible AI team at Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, based in New York. I work on making LLM agents safer. More broadly, I work on evaluation, and on finding out why AI systems fail and how to fix them.

Research interests

i.Safeguards for LLM agentsDefending agentic and multi-agent systems against prompt injection, unsafe actions and hallucinated tool calls.

These days I mostly work on safeguards for agentic and multi-agent systems, across different attack surfaces and attack types. In these systems, malicious instructions can arrive through user input, but also through tool outputs, tool descriptions or messages from other agents. I also work on catching unsafe actions and hallucinated tool calls. Our recent paper, MAS-Guard, proposes guardrails that work for both single agents and multi-agent systems arXiv '26. Before Oracle, I led the prompt-injection, toxic-content and hallucination detectors that shipped in Amazon Bedrock Guardrails. On the side, I've been exploring automated red-teaming where the attacker and the guardrail keep improving against each other.

ii.EvaluationEvaluation data, pipelines and benchmarks, for production systems and for research.

A lot of my work has been about evaluation, both for production systems and in published research. At AWS I worked with human annotators to collect high-quality seed data for Bedrock Guardrails and for RAG post-training of Amazon Titan models, and built pipelines that generate synthetic data from it for fine-tuning and evaluation. At Oracle I run the evaluation pipeline for our agent safeguards, including synthetic data for measuring vulnerabilities and stress-testing safeguards before release.

I've also worked on benchmarks such as BBQ for social bias in question answering ACL Findings '22, (QA)² for questions with questionable assumptions ACL '23, and open-domain QA with conflicting retrieved contexts NAACL Findings '25. Beyond building them, I've looked at how good our benchmarks are, using item response theory to compare test sets ACL '21 and to cluster examples across benchmarks Insights '22. During my PhD I helped build jiant, a toolkit for training and evaluating NLU models, and I co-organized the IWSLT 2023 shared task on formality control in translation IWSLT '23.

iii.Why AI systems work or failAnalyzing why models, agents and multi-agent systems fail, and fixing what we find.

I like figuring out why a model, or a whole agentic system around it, behaves the way it does, and then using that to fix it. While post-training Titan models at AWS, I explored methods for estimating P("I know") and used them in ablation experiments to design fine-tuning data that teaches a model to say it doesn't know, ask a follow-up question, or call retrieval, building on work by Kadavath et al. (2022) showing that models largely know what they know. With PhD interns, I've also worked on long-context hallucination detection NAACL Findings '25, estimating black-box uncertainty by probing a model with varied rephrasings EMNLP Findings '25, and checking whether vision-language models' reasoning steps are grounded in the image, and repairing those that aren't EACL '26.

My PhD work had a similar flavor. I studied when and why intermediate-task transfer helps pretrained models ACL '20 AACL '20, what BERT knows about grammar EMNLP '19 and whether its attention heads track syntax arXiv '19, and replicated a grammar induction result to understand why it worked EMNLP '18.
